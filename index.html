A bit about me

I'm a student at the High School Affiliated to Beijing Normal University in Beijing, spending most of my time between a chemistry lab and a video editing timeline.

My research started with a question about catalysts — how tiny amounts of platinum can transform waste glycerol into something useful. That question led me to a Peking University lab, a first-author manuscript now under review, and eventually to the Tengger Desert, where I discovered that the biggest catalyst isn't always a molecule.

When I'm not in the lab, I'm probably on my bike — 50-plus kilometres in most weeks, with a few 200-kilometre single-day rides each year. Some of my best ideas about reaction mechanisms came somewhere around kilometer 40.

In the Lab

From a Peking University laboratory to an international research platform — building depth in catalysis, materials, and computational chemistry.

National Yingcai Program · Peking University · 2025

Glycerol Oxidation Catalysis

Selected for China's national Yingcai (Talents) Program, co-administered by the China Association for Science and Technology and the Ministry of Education — roughly 1,800 students nationwide per academic year, trained across 58 partner universities.

In a laboratory affiliated with Peking University's College of Chemistry and Molecular Engineering, I independently operated XRD, TEM, and XPS to characterize Pt–Fe–CeO₂ catalysts for glycerol oxidation. The resulting first-author manuscript is now under review at NHSJS.

Tsinghua University · Yau Science Award Candidate

GreenBatty — Greener Battery Electrolytes

A Tsinghua-based research program asking whether five natural polyphenols can serve as electrolyte additives in a fixed lithium-battery chemistry. Candidate molecules are locked into a dual-constraint test matrix; AI assists pre-unblinding triage while wet-lab validation rounds continue. Yau Science Award submission in preparation.
